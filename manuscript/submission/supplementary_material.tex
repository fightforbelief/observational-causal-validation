\section{Cross-fit scores and the second bootstrap}
\label{sec:scores}

On an estimation sample of size $n$, let $D_i$ be the treatment indicator, $Y_i$ the observed outcome, $e_i$ the out-of-fold propensity score, and $(\mu_{1i},\mu_{0i})$ the out-of-fold arm-specific outcome predictions. Clipping replaces $e_i$ by its projection onto $[10^{-6},\,1-10^{-6}]$. For the average treatment effect the cross-fit score of \citet{bang2005} is
\begin{equation}
(\mu_{1i}-\mu_{0i})
+
\frac{D_i}{e_i}(Y_i-\mu_{1i})
-
\frac{1-D_i}{1-e_i}(Y_i-\mu_{0i}).
\label{eq:ate-score}
\end{equation}
For the average treatment effect on the treated, with $p$ held at the mean of $D$ in the estimation sample,
\begin{equation}
\frac{D_i}{p}(Y_i-\mu_{0i})
-
\frac{(1-D_i)\,e_i}{p\,(1-e_i)}(Y_i-\mu_{0i}).
\label{eq:att-score}
\end{equation}
For the untreated effect, reverse the treatment labels in the treated score and negate the result to retain the treatment-minus-control sign. Folds are ordinary $K$-fold partitions with $K=5$, not stratified folds. In the prespecified fit the random seed is 20261026. The reported analytic standard error uses the raw scores, with divisor $n-1$ inside the standard deviation. A recentered influence function that treats $p$ as estimated is a different standard error; it was not the primary reported standard error, and it is not the bootstrap standard deviation.

The independent implementation reproduced the primary observational point estimate to within $1.3\times 10^{-15}$ and the primary randomized estimate exactly. Its four-cell bootstrap is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:impl}. The contrast standard deviation is about 0.470, and the percentile interval is about $[-0.925,\,0.661]$. This bootstrap redraws G1, G2, G3, and G4, whereas the prespecified bootstrap redraws only G1, G2, and G4. The random-number streams also differ, and the bootstrap distribution of the observational treated effect has a heavy tail. The interval is an implementation-specific sensitivity result. Table~\ref{tab:exact-primary} is unchanged by it.

In that margin, 41 replicates of the observational treated estimate are negative and one is below $-2$. The minimum is about $-8.62$, and the excess kurtosis is 163.25. The isolated minimum contributes to the bootstrap standard deviation. The 2.5th percentile is determined by replicates near rank 50. Dividing the interquartile range by 1.349 gives 0.307, which describes the center of the distribution only. The untreated and average-effect margins do not have a comparable single-replicate spike; their excess kurtosis is about 4.
